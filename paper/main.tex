\title{Monotone Recurrence Has a Blind Spot: Diagnosis and\\
Repair for Temporal Knowledge Graph Forecasting}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Institution} \\ \textit{Address} \\ email}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Recurrence dominates temporal knowledge graph (TKG) forecasting: a plain
recurrency baseline matches RE-GCN, CyGNet, TiRGN and L2-TKG on the standard
benchmarks. Every recurrence mechanism in this literature scores a historical
candidate by a function of how often it occurred and how long ago, which is
non-decreasing in the first and non-increasing in the second --- whether the
two are multiplied, as in the copy and decay terms of CyGNet, CENET, TiRGN,
RE-GCN and DaeMon and in the learned exponential kernel of the neural-Hawkes
model GAttNHP, or added, as in the rule confidences of CountTRuCoLa. We show
that this entire family is provably unable to rank a large and directly
measurable share of queries: whenever a distractor has both smaller elapsed
time and larger count than the answer, no such scorer can place the answer
first, however its parameters are fitted. We call such queries \emph{monotone-blocked}, note that the
property is defined without reference to any model, and measure it at
$41.6\%$ of YAGO, $29.3\%$ of WIKI, $26.6\%$ of ICEWS18 and $15.8\%$ of GDELT
test queries, and it partitions any test set into strata on which a mechanism
can be tested rather than only scored. We then introduce \textsc{Cadence},
which abandons the two-argument form altogether: its recurrence branch is an
unconstrained function of thirteen inter-arrival statistics of a candidate's
observed history, conditioned on the query, so it is neither monotone in
$\Delta t$ nor a function of the count and the elapsed time alone, and it
recovers the classical exponential kernel as one point in its hypothesis
space. The two branches are combined as what they are --- intensities of
superposed point processes --- through a log-sum-exp rather than a learned
gate. Restricting the branch to the published monotone form while holding the
backbone, the optimiser and the schedule fixed costs $1.83$, $8.11$, $5.39$
and $4.57$ MRR on the four benchmarks, and the loss concentrates on the
blocked stratum, where H@1 falls by $5.66$ on YAGO and $18.31$ on WIKI against
$1.77$ and $6.61$ on unblocked queries with a history. \textsc{Cadence} is at
or above the strongest numbers reported by the comparison tables it is
measured against, under a time-aware filtered protocol we verify against the
reference implementation; we also document why those tables disagree with each
other and what that does to any leaderboard claim in this area, our own
included.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A temporal knowledge graph records facts that hold at a time, as quadruples
$(s,r,o,t)$. Forecasting asks which object completes $(s,r,?,t)$ using only
what happened strictly before $t$ --- who a state will next sanction, which
office a person will next hold --- and it is the setting in which such graphs
are actually deployed.

The dominant signal in this task is repetition. A large fraction of the facts
to be predicted have occurred before with the same subject and relation, and
the field has documented how far that alone goes: a recurrency baseline that
simply ranks candidates by how often and how recently they co-occurred with
$(s,r)$ is competitive with several published
architectures~\cite{historyrepeats,cygnet}. Every strong system since has
included such a term. CyGNet~\cite{cygnet} copies from a frequency vector over
the history; CENET~\cite{cenet} adds a contrastive historical / non-historical
oracle on top of one; TiRGN~\cite{tirgn} and RE-GCN~\cite{regcn} attach one to
a recurrent structural encoder; DaeMon~\cite{daemon} carries one alongside
path evidence; GAttNHP~\cite{gattnhp} recasts the task as a neural Hawkes
process and learns a per-entity decay rate.

The interesting question is therefore not whether to model repetition, but
what the models we already have \emph{cannot express}. Read as scorers, all of
the above assign a historical candidate $o$ a value determined by two
quantities --- how often the fact occurred, $c_o$, and how long ago it last
occurred, $\Delta t_o$ --- through a function that rises in the first and falls
in the second. Most of them multiply a count term by a decay term; the recent
rule-learning method CountTRuCoLa~\cite{counttrucola} adds them instead. The
parameterisations differ, and GAttNHP fits the decay rate rather than fixing
it, but that shared monotonicity is common to all of them, and it is the
monotonicity that binds.

This paper's starting point is that the shape has a blind spot which is
provable in one line and, more importantly, is \emph{measurable without
reference to any model}. If some distractor occurred both more often and more
recently than the true answer, then no scorer with that monotonicity can rank
the answer above it, whatever its parameters: both of the quantities it reads
already favour the distractor (Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked}). We call such a query \emph{monotone-blocked}.
The definition mentions only the query and the observed histories of its
candidates, so the share of blocked queries is a property of a dataset rather
than of a system, and it can be counted directly. On the four standard
benchmarks it is $41.6\%$ (YAGO), $29.3\%$ (WIKI), $26.6\%$ (ICEWS18) and
$15.8\%$ (GDELT) of test queries.

A fair objection is that no published system uses its recurrence term in
isolation: each pairs it with a structural encoder that is not subject to the
bound and can rescue a blocked query. That is true, and it is why we do not
claim these systems fail on those queries. What the measurement establishes is
narrower and still consequential: on between a sixth and two-fifths of all
queries, the component that carries the dominant signal of the task is
structurally unable to produce the right ordering and must hand the query to
the branch that is weaker on exactly this kind of data. That is a large amount
of delegation to design in on purpose.

Our response is to stop repairing the product and leave it. The recurrence
branch of \textsc{Cadence} summarises a candidate's observed history by
thirteen inter-arrival statistics --- counts, elapsed times and mean gaps for
the $(s,r,o)$, $(s,\cdot,o)$ and $(r,\cdot,o)$ views, plus gap dispersion,
span, rate and a has-history flag --- and maps them through an unconstrained
network. Nothing in it is monotone in $\Delta t$, nothing is separable into a
count factor and a time factor, and the classical count-times-decay scorer is
one point in its hypothesis space, so the generalisation is strict. Holding the
backbone, the optimiser and the schedule fixed and restricting only this branch
to the published form costs $1.83$, $8.11$, $5.39$ and $4.57$ MRR on the four
benchmarks: the functional form, not the encoder, carries the gain.

Two further choices follow from taking the point-process reading
seriously~\cite{daleyvere}. Since the two branches are intensities of
superposed processes, their intensities \emph{add}, so they must be combined by
a log-sum-exp; summing logits, as a gated architecture effectively does,
multiplies intensities instead, which has no meaning under the model and lets
one branch rescale the other. And because superposition prescribes the
combination, there is no mixing weight to learn --- hence no gate that can
collapse onto a single branch, a failure we observed directly in earlier gated
variants of this work.

Because our margins over the state of the art are in places smaller than the
differences between evaluation conventions in this literature, we verified our
harness against the RE-GCN reference implementation on all four datasets before
reporting anything (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked}, which identifies a provable blind spot
        shared by every recurrence mechanism in this literature, and the
        model-free diagnostic it induces --- a dataset statistic that can be
        computed before any model is trained.
  \item \textsc{Cadence}, whose recurrence intensity strictly contains the
        family the proposition constrains, together with a superposition rule
        that follows from the point-process reading and admits no gate.
  \item A stratified evaluation that tests the proposed mechanism on the
        stratum it targets rather than reporting only a pooled metric. This is
        where we ask the paper to be judged: the restriction to the monotone
        form costs $5.66$ H@1 on YAGO's blocked stratum against $1.77$ on its
        unblocked queries, and $18.31$ against $6.61$ on WIKI.
  \item Results on YAGO, WIKI, ICEWS18 and GDELT under a time-aware filtered
        protocol verified against the reference implementation rather than
        assumed to match it --- together with an account, in
        Section~\ref{sec:comparability}, of why published tables in this area
        disagree and what that costs any leaderboard claim, ours included.
  \item Two negative results reported in full, including one mechanism we
        expected to work and removed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Recurrence and copy mechanisms}
CyGNet~\cite{cygnet} scores candidates from a copy vector of frequencies over
the observed history. CENET~\cite{cenet} pairs a frequency scorer with an
explicit historical / non-historical oracle trained contrastively.
TiRGN~\cite{tirgn} and RE-GCN~\cite{regcn} attach a history vector to a
recurrent structural encoder, and DaeMon~\cite{daemon} carries one alongside
path evidence. Gastinger et al.~\cite{historyrepeats} showed that a baseline
consisting of nothing but such a term is competitive with eleven published
methods on five datasets, which is the empirical fact this paper starts from.
All of these score a candidate by a quantity that rises with the count and
falls with the elapsed time, so all fall under
Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked}.

\paragraph{Rule-based forecasting}
TLogic-style rule induction and, most recently,
CountTRuCoLa~\cite{counttrucola} learn interpretable temporal rules whose
confidence is fitted per rule. CountTRuCoLa is the closest published work to
our diagnosis, because it also treats the shape of the recency response as the
object to be learned rather than fixed. It differs in two ways that matter
here. Its confidence \emph{adds} a recency term to a frequency term instead of
multiplying them, which is why we state
Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked} at the level of monotonicity rather than for
products; and its recency term is an exponential in the elapsed time with a
fitted rate, so the learned object stays inside the monotone family. It is
also the strongest published result we are aware of on GDELT under the
time-aware filtered protocol, and we include it in
Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\paragraph{Point processes for TKG}
GAttNHP~\cite{gattnhp} casts TKG extrapolation as a neural Hawkes process with
base, self- and group-excitation, but its kernel is $\exp(-\gamma_u \Delta t)$
with a learned per-entity rate, and is therefore inside the constrained family.
We adopt the point-process reading itself --- it is what dictates our
superposition rule~\cite{daleyvere} --- and claim no novelty for it; our
contribution concerns the shape of the kernel.

\paragraph{Structural encoders}
RE-GCN~\cite{regcn} evolves entity representations over snapshots with an
R-GCN and a GRU. XERTE~\cite{xerte} and TITer~\cite{titer} instead search an
explicit subgraph or trajectory. DiMNet~\cite{dimnet} adds cross-time
perception at equal hop depth and disentangles node features into active and
stable factors; its ablation attributes $-11.38$ MRR to disentanglement,
$-9.62$ to virtual-subgraph refinement and $-4.97$ to multi-span.
\emph{We adopt multi-span evolution and active/stable disentanglement as our
backbone and claim no novelty for them}; our decoder is
ConvTransE~\cite{convtranse}. Concurrent unrefereed work~\cite{est} proposes a
persistent global entity-state buffer with fast and slow tracks, which is a
more expressive version of the stability mechanism we inherit from DiMNet; its
reported figures on ICEWS18 and GDELT are more than ten MRR points above every
other number in this literature, including its own baselines, so we do not
treat them as comparable to ours and do not tabulate them.

\paragraph{Path-based reasoning}
DaeMon~\cite{daemon} carries an NBFNet-style~\cite{nbfnet} query-conditioned
state across snapshots with a time-aware gate and contains no entity
embeddings, so a never-seen candidate is still scored on path evidence.
TiPNN~\cite{tipnn} builds a query-aware history graph in the same spirit. We
reimplement this as an optional third intensity and report it in
Section~\ref{sec:negative} as a negative result at our scale.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol}
Published numbers use incompatible settings, and Gastinger et
al.~\cite{gastingereval} showed that the choice alone moves results enough to
reverse rankings between methods. RE-GCN, TiRGN, CENET, DaeMon, TiPNN, DiMNet
and CountTRuCoLa report time-aware filtered; GAttNHP reports raw. We report all
three settings, compare only within one, and additionally verify our
implementation of the time-aware filter against the reference code
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

A TKG is a set of quadruples $(s,r,o,t)$. Forecasting asks for $o$ given
$(s,r,?,t)$ using only facts strictly before $t$. We report MRR and
Hits@$\{1,3,10\}$. Ties are resolved to their average position,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{rank} = 1 + \#\{\text{strictly better}\}
              + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\#\{\text{tied}\} - 1\bigr),
\end{equation}
because counting only strictly-better scores inflates every metric for models
that assign many candidates an identical score. Under that convention an early
copy-only variant of our own model reported $\mathrm{H@}10 = 99.93$ on YAGO,
against $93.34$ for DaeMon.

\section{The Blind Spot of Monotone Recurrence}
\label{sec:blocked}

\subsection{Proposition}

Published recurrence terms differ in how they put a count and an elapsed time
together. Most multiply, $f(c)\,g(\Delta t)$; the recent rule-learning method
CountTRuCoLa~\cite{counttrucola} instead \emph{adds} a recency term and a
frequency term. Stating the bound for the product form alone would therefore
leave an obvious escape, so we state it at the level of monotonicity, where no
such escape exists.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:blocked}
Let a recurrence scorer be any function
$s(o) = \psi(c_o, \Delta t_o)$ of the number of past occurrences $c_o$ of the
candidate and the time $\Delta t_o$ since its last occurrence, such that
$\psi$ is non-decreasing in its first argument and non-increasing in its
second. Let $o^\star$ be the answer and let a distractor $o$ satisfy
$c_o \ge c_{o^\star}$ and $\Delta t_o \le \Delta t_{o^\star}$. Then
$s(o) \ge s(o^\star)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\psi(c_o, \Delta t_o) \ge \psi(c_{o^\star}, \Delta t_o)$ by monotonicity in
the first argument, and
$\psi(c_{o^\star}, \Delta t_o) \ge \psi(c_{o^\star}, \Delta t_{o^\star})$ by
monotonicity in the second. Chaining the two gives the claim.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis is weak, which is the point. It asks only that seeing a fact
more often does not lower its score and that seeing it longer ago does not
raise it --- the two properties every recurrence mechanism in this literature
is designed to have. It is satisfied by any product $f(c)\,g(\Delta t)$ with
$f$ non-decreasing and $g$ positive and non-increasing, by any sum
$f(c) + g(\Delta t)$ of the same, and more generally by $h(f(c), g(\Delta t))$
for any $h$ non-decreasing in both arguments. It therefore covers the copy
vector of CyGNet~\cite{cygnet}, the historical scorers of
CENET~\cite{cenet}, TiRGN~\cite{tirgn}, RE-GCN~\cite{regcn} and
DaeMon~\cite{daemon}, the additive rule confidences of
CountTRuCoLa~\cite{counttrucola}, and the exponential kernel of
GAttNHP~\cite{gattnhp} --- whose per-entity decay rate is learned, but learned
inside the hypothesis. No choice of decay rate and no reweighting of counts
ranks $o^\star$ strictly first.

We call such a query \emph{monotone-blocked}. The definition involves only the
query and the observed histories of its candidates, and no model, so it is a
statistic of a dataset and can be computed before anything is trained.

\paragraph{The two ways out}
Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked} leaves exactly two escapes, and we take both. A
scorer may fail to be monotone in the two arguments, or it may not be a
function of those two arguments alone. The branch of
Section~\ref{sec:method} reads thirteen statistics rather than two, and is
unconstrained in all of them.

\paragraph{Scope of the bound}
Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked} constrains a recurrence scorer \emph{in
isolation}. Published systems pair such a term with a structural branch that
is not subject to it and can rescue a blocked query. What
Table~\ref{tab:blocked} establishes is that on $15.8$--$41.6\%$ of queries the
recurrence component cannot produce the correct ordering and must delegate.

\subsection{Two regimes}

Table~\ref{tab:blocked} separates the benchmarks into two regimes, and the rest
of the paper is read against that split. On YAGO and WIKI almost every answer
has a history ($92.8\%$, $87.0\%$), so recurrence is where the task is decided
and the blocked share of \emph{all} queries is correspondingly large. On
ICEWS18 and GDELT most answers have no history at all ($47.7\%$ and $24.9\%$
do), so the blocked share of all queries is smaller --- but conditional on
having a history it is \emph{higher}, $55.7\%$ and $63.4\%$. The blind spot is
therefore not confined to the slowly-evolving datasets; on the event datasets
it is denser, over a smaller pool. Section~\ref{sec:strat} evaluates on these
strata separately rather than reporting only the pooled metric.

\section{\textsc{Cadence}}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includesvg[width=\textwidth]{architecture}
\caption{Overview of the model. \emph{Left}: the structural branch. Each
historical snapshot $\mathcal{G}(t_i)$ is passed through three multi-span GCN
layers whose message at depth $\ell$ also reads depth $\ell$ at $t_{i-1}$, is
disentangled into an active and a stable factor, and is carried across time by a
GRU, giving the evolved entity table $E(t)$. A ConvTransE decoder scores every
entity against $[E(t)[s]\,\|\,R[r]]$. \emph{Right}: the recurrence branch, drawn
in full because it is where the contribution lies. For each candidate $o$ we read
its observed occurrence history off a precomputed CSR index and summarise it by
thirteen inter-arrival statistics, which an unconstrained MLP maps to a scalar.
The two timelines $o^{\star}$ and $o'$ are the configuration of
Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked}: the distractor has both the smaller $\Delta t$
and the larger count, so every score of the form $f(\text{count})\cdot g(\Delta
t)$ with $g$ non-increasing ranks it at least as high as the answer, whatever
decay rate is fitted. \emph{Bottom}: the branches are point-process intensities,
so they are superposed by $\log\lambda = \mathrm{logaddexp}(f_{\mathrm{struct}},
f_{\mathrm{rec}})$ on the candidate set and by $f_{\mathrm{struct}}$ elsewhere.
No mixing weight is learned, so there is no gate that can collapse onto one
branch.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Superposition, not gating}

Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} gives the whole model. A query is a draw from a
superposition of two marked point processes on the entity space,
\begin{equation}
\lambda(o) = \lambda_{\mathrm{struct}}(o \mid \mathcal{G}_{<t}, s, r)
           + \lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}(o \mid \mathcal{H}_o, s, r).
\end{equation}
For a superposition the probability that the next mark is $o$ is
$\lambda(o)/\sum_{o'}\lambda(o')$, so a softmax cross-entropy on
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{score}(o) = \log \lambda(o) =
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{logaddexp}\bigl(f_{\mathrm{struct}}(o), f_{\mathrm{rec}}(o)\bigr) & o \in S,\\
f_{\mathrm{struct}}(o) & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
is the correct discrete-choice likelihood. Two consequences matter in
practice. Superposition \emph{adds intensities}, so the branches combine
through $\mathrm{logaddexp}$ and not through a sum of logits; a sum of logits
multiplies intensities, which has no point-process meaning and allows one
branch to rescale the other. And there is no mixing gate, hence no gate to
collapse: earlier variants of this work with a learned scalar or per-query
gate collapsed to a single branch within a few epochs.

\subsection{The recurrence intensity}

The proposition constrains a functional \emph{form}, not a parameterisation, so
no amount of fitting inside that form escapes it. We therefore leave the form.
For a candidate $o$ we summarise its observed history by a vector
$\phi_o \in \mathbb{R}^{13}$ and set
\begin{equation}
\log \lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}(o) =
\mathrm{MLP}\bigl[\,\mathrm{LN}(\phi_o) \,\|\, u(r) \,\|\, v(o)
                  \,\|\, q(E(t)[s])\,\bigr] + b,
\end{equation}
where $u$, $v$ and $q$ are learned relation, entity and subject contexts, the
trunk is two Linear--LayerNorm--GELU blocks, and $b$ is a scalar initialised at
$-2$.

The subject term $q$ is not decoration, and leaving it out has a specific
cost. Because $\mathrm{logaddexp}(a,b) \ge a$, the recurrence intensity can
only ever raise a candidate that has a history relative to one that does not.
Its only way to stay out of the way on a query whose answer never occurred
with $(s,r)$ is to drive its own intensity down --- and it cannot decide to do
that without seeing the query it is answering. This is not a gate between the
branches: no mixing weight is learned and the superposition rule of
Section~\ref{sec:method} is untouched. It is the intensity depending on the
conditioning information $\lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}(o \mid \mathcal{H}_o, s, r)$
was defined on.

The thirteen statistics are the count, the elapsed time $\Delta t$ and the mean
inter-arrival gap of each of the $(s,r,o)$, $(s,\cdot,o)$ and $(r,\cdot,o)$
histories, together with the dispersion of the gaps, the span and rate of the
$(s,r,o)$ history, and a binary has-history indicator. They are read from a
compressed sparse-row temporal index built once per dataset and carry no
gradient, so the branch adds no message passing to the backbone.

Two properties matter. First, the family in
Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked} is contained in this one: taking the MLP to
compute $\log a + p\log(1+c) - b\,\Delta t$ recovers the classical
count-times-decay scorer exactly, so we cannot lose by generalising, and
Section~\ref{sec:mech} measures what we gain by doing so. Second, nothing
forces the score to decrease in $\Delta t$ or to increase in the count, and it
is a joint function of both together with seven further statistics --- so the
blocked configuration of Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked} is reachable, which is
precisely what the product form forbids.

\subsection{Structural intensity}

The backbone is adopted from prior work: a composition GCN over each snapshot
with a cross-time link at equal layer depth, aggregating with both mean and max
--- a reduced form of the multi-aggregator scheme of~\cite{pna}, which
separates many weak neighbours from one decisive one where a mean cannot ---
node states split into
complementary active and stable factors with a drift penalty on the stable
factor across adjacent snapshots, a GRU across snapshots, and a ConvTransE
decoder over the full entity table. We use LayerNorm rather than BatchNorm in
the decoder: batches here are snapshots, so batch size is however many facts
occurred at one timestamp and varies from $72$ to $4{,}650$ across our
datasets, and once a long snapshot is split into query chunks the result would
otherwise depend on the split.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Four benchmarks, time-aware filtered protocol, baselines quoted from the
DaeMon table (YAGO, WIKI) and the DiMNet table (ICEWS18, GDELT), both of which
report that protocol. $d=200$, three GCN layers, history of ten snapshots
(five on GDELT), $5.96$M parameters. One A100 per run: YAGO 42\,min, ICEWS18
1\,h\,15, WIKI 3\,h, GDELT 8\,h.

\subsection{The protocol is verified against the reference}
\label{sec:protocol}

A subtle difference in filtering would invalidate margins of the size we
report, so we checked our harness against RE-GCN's \texttt{rgcn/utils.py},
which is the de-facto standard for this benchmark family. The reference
appends inverse triples in \texttt{predict()} and scores both directions; so
do we, and the scored query counts are identical on all four datasets. The
reference sets every other true answer at the query timestamp to $-10^7$ and
restores the target; we do the same with $-\infty$. The reference builds the
filter from the test split alone whereas we build it from all splits; the
filter is keyed by timestamp, so the two coincide when splits are temporally
disjoint. We verified disjointness on the actual files --- zero overlapping
timestamps on all four datasets --- and then verified directly that the two
answer sets agree on $4{,}000$ sampled test queries per dataset, with zero
differences.

One difference remains. The reference ranks by sort position, so a target tied
with $k-1$ others lands anywhere in that group; we take the average position,
which is the expectation of that draw. Ours is therefore unbiased with respect
to the reference and cannot be optimistic.

\subsection{What these margins are and are not worth}
\label{sec:comparability}

We report Table~\ref{tab:main} because the field expects it, and we then argue
that it should not carry the weight of the paper. Three facts, none of them
ours, make that position hard to avoid.

First, the comparison tables disagree with each other.
Table~\ref{tab:main} follows DiMNet's table on the event datasets and DaeMon's
on YAGO and WIKI. DiMNet's table does not contain LogCL, TiRGN, CENET or
CountTRuCoLa. LogCL~\cite{logcl} reports ICEWS18 MRR $35.67$, above both
DiMNet's $34.13$ and ours, a year before DiMNet claimed the state of the art;
CognTKE~\cite{cogntke} reports WIKI $83.21$, above ours. Both state that they
use the time-aware filtered protocol, as do we. They cannot all be measuring
the same quantity, and we have not rerun them, so we do not claim to beat
them. Gastinger et al.~\cite{gastingereval} documented precisely this and
showed the setting alone can reverse rankings.

Second, the quantity being compared is partly a shortcut. A baseline that
ranks candidates by $(s,r)$ co-occurrence frequency and uses no temporal
information at all comes within roughly a quarter of the state of the art on
YAGO and WIKI and within $15\%$ on GDELT~\cite{shortcut}, and the non-learned
recurrency baseline of~\cite{historyrepeats} outscores most published models on
GDELT and YAGO outright. Against that background our YAGO margin of $+0.05$
MRR over DaeMon is not evidence of anything.

Third, and consequently, a pooled metric cannot separate a better mechanism
from a better fit to the shortcut. That is what
Section~\ref{sec:strat} is for, and it is where we ask the paper to be judged:
the blocked stratum is by construction the subset on which a monotone
count-and-recency scorer cannot succeed, so a gain concentrated there is
evidence about the mechanism in a way that a pooled MRR is not.

\subsection{The mechanism ablation}
\label{sec:mech}

The claim is about a functional form, so the ablation replaces the form and
nothing else. We restrict the recurrence branch to exactly the published
scorer, $\log a(r,o) + p(r,o)\log(1+c) - b(r,o)\,\Delta t$ with $p, b \ge 0$
predicted from $(r,o)$ alone, and hold the backbone, the superposition rule,
the optimiser and the schedule fixed. A unit test sweeps $\Delta t$ and $c$ over
twenty seeds and asserts that the restricted branch is monotone in both and
that the full branch is not.

\subsection{Where the gain lands}
\label{sec:strat}

A pooled metric cannot say whether a gain came from the mechanism this paper
argues for. We therefore partition the test set into three disjoint strata:
\emph{blocked}, where a distractor dominates the answer in both $\Delta t$ and
count; \emph{clean}, where the answer has a history and is not dominated; and
\emph{no-history}, where the answer never occurred with $(s,r)$ before. Only
the first is the target of Proposition~\ref{prop:blocked}.

\section{Two Negative Results}
\label{sec:negative}

\paragraph{Deep supervision on the structural branch hurts}
Paired on three seeds, adding a cross-entropy on the structural scores alone
costs $0.87$ MRR and $1.66$ H@1 on YAGO, in the same direction on every seed,
and raises the seed standard deviation from $0.04$ to $0.42$. It was
introduced to stop recurrence from starving the structural branch; on a
$92.8\%$-recurrent dataset that trade is not worth making.

\paragraph{A path branch does not pay at our scale}
We reimplemented DaeMon's query-conditioned propagation as a third intensity,
with no entity embeddings. It doubled H@1 on the stratum where the answer has
no $(s,r)$ history ($0.79 \rightarrow 1.68$ on YAGO) --- exactly the stratum
it targets --- and still lost overall ($91.46$ against $91.64$ MRR), because
that stratum is only $7.3\%$ of YAGO. Its state is
(queries $\times$ entities $\times$ dim), roughly $50\times$ the arithmetic of
the other branches.

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{The no-history stratum}
The stratum where the answer has no $(s,r)$ history is the largest single pool
of failures on the event datasets: $50.1\%$ of ICEWS18 at $5.65$ H@1 and
$40.4\%$ of GDELT at $1.63$. Query conditioning lets the recurrence intensity
step back on such queries, but it cannot supply evidence the branch does not
have; the structural branch is weak there and we do not close this.

\paragraph{Baselines are quoted, not rerun}
Section~\ref{sec:protocol} removes the filtering and query-count risk in
\emph{our} harness, but it says nothing about theirs. Since published tables in
this area disagree (Section~\ref{sec:comparability}), the honest statement is
that we match or exceed the tables we are measured against and that no
cross-table claim in this literature, ours included, is currently safe. Rerunning
LogCL~\cite{logcl} and one 2026 method under our harness is the obvious next
step and is not done here.

\paragraph{Benchmarks}
All four datasets are known to be partly solvable by a co-occurrence
shortcut~\cite{shortcut,historyrepeats}. Our stratified evaluation is a
response to this, not a solution to it; the bias-corrected replacements
proposed in~\cite{shortcut} appeared too recently for us to use, and the
diagnostic of Section~\ref{sec:blocked} applies unchanged to them.

\paragraph{Other}
H@10 is below the compared systems on three datasets. We report three seeds,
and one on GDELT.
